Research

Understanding Task Superposition in Large Language Models

Independent · 2026

Sole author · formal title: Beyond First-Order Task Vector Superposition: Orthogonal Residuals in In-Context Learning

Do mixed in-context tasks actually appear inside a transformer as a linear combination of task-specific representations?

Give a transformer a mix of in-context tasks and it behaves as though it is holding all of them at once. That result is behavioral, and what I wanted to know is whether it is literally true of the representations — whether the model's residual stream really does carry a linear superposition of the individual task vectors, or whether the behavior arises some other way. This is an activation-level test of that claim across five instruction-tuned models spanning three families and 1B–8B parameters (Llama-3.2-1B/3B, Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen-2.5-3B, Gemma-2-2B).

  • Geometry. Mixed-task centroids land on the ratio-weighted convex combination of pure-task centroids (κ ≈ 1), significant at the floor p = 1/720 of an exact permutation null for every model, task group, and layer.
  • The orthogonal residual. The component κ is blind to, calibrated against a split-half sampling-noise floor: its magnitude grows with depth while its resolvability above the floor peaks in the middle layers, yielding three regimes governed by task separability.
  • Causal ablation. Surgically removing each ratio's systematic residual shifts the next-token distribution far more than a norm-matched random orthogonal direction — showing magnitude, resolvability, and causal importance are three distinct properties.

Comparative Evaluation of Vision-Based Models for Histopathological Breast Cancer Classification

MDST · Winter 2025

Team project · main contributor, Vision Transformer

The BreakHis biopsy slides carry two labels at once: benign or malignant, and one of eight finer histological subtypes. A six-person team asked whether making a model learn the subtypes first improves the coarse call that matters clinically, and trained four architectures twice each — once on the binary label, once on the subtypes with predictions collapsed afterwards — so that label granularity was the only thing varying. I built the Vision Transformer arm and wrote up the cross-architecture comparison. It was a semester-long student project rather than a full study, but it was the first time I designed an experiment where the comparison, not the model, was the point.
