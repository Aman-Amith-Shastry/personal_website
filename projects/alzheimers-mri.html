Alzheimer's Detection from MRI Scans

Michigan Data Science Team · 2025 · project co-lead

PyTorchtorchvisionVGG-16ResNet-50Grad-CAMStreamlit

Overview

Staging Alzheimer's from axial MRI across four levels of impairment — none, very mild, mild, and moderate — instead of the usual healthy-versus-diseased split.

We chose the harder framing on purpose. The boundaries worth getting right clinically are the early ones, and they are exactly the ones a binary model never has to learn: a classifier that only separates healthy from diseased is free to lump very mild in with either side and still look excellent. Four classes forces the question of whether the features actually distinguish early decline from a healthy brain.

It was also the project I taught from. Most of my time on it went into walking members through CNNs, residual-network fine-tuning, feature visualization, and ensembling, and writing the notebooks they worked from. Each member trained their own architecture end to end rather than inheriting a pipeline, and we compared the best result from each at the end — slower, but they left having actually built something.

How it works

Data
A public Kaggle collection of axial MRI slices, labeled across the four impairment levels and balanced across classes in the training split.
Models
VGG-16 and ResNet-50 from torchvision, each with its final classifier replaced by a four-output layer — the last entry of VGG's classifier block, the fully connected head on the ResNet — then fine-tuned. Running both on identical splits is the point: it asks whether the extra depth and the residual connections buy anything at this dataset size, or whether the older, wider network is enough.
Preprocessing
One shared transform so the comparison stays honest: grayscale expanded to three channels, resize to 256, center crop to 224, ImageNet normalization.
Attribution
A Grad-CAM implementation that registers forward and backward hooks on the last convolutional block of whichever architecture it is handed — the final conv layer in VGG's feature stack, the last residual stage in the ResNet — and produces a heatmap over the slice. This is the check that matters on medical images: a model can score well while keying on the skull outline or a scanner artifact, and the heatmap is how you catch it.
Demo
A Streamlit app so members can drop in a scan and see the prediction and its heatmap side by side without touching the training code.
