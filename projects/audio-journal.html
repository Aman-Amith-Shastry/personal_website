Overview

A journaling app you talk to instead of type into. The idea the team got interested in was reading sentiment out of the audio itself rather than a transcript — how something is said carries more than the words do.

Transcribe an entry and you throw away the pauses, the pitch, the flatness of a bad day. So the model never sees text at all: it works on the waveform, and the app tracks how entries move over time rather than scoring any single one. Alongside the sentiment prediction the service returns a handful of prosodic measurements, which turned out to be the part people found most legible — a label is arguable, but "you spoke faster and quieter this week" is something you can check against your own memory.

With ten people on it, most of my own work was structural: splitting the group across the model, backend, and app layers, reshuffling as scope shifted, and keeping the three from drifting apart.

How it works

Model
WavLM-base from HuggingFace transformers with a classification head on top of the pooled hidden states — a linear layer down to 512, ReLU, dropout, then a projection to the emotion labels — fine-tuned end to end on speech sentiment. Weights live on the HuggingFace Hub and are pulled at container start rather than baked into the image.
Audio pipeline
Entries arrive as recordings from the phone, which means .m4a. The service decodes them in memory with pydub, downmixes to mono, resamples to the 16 kHz WavLM expects with librosa, then runs the Wav2Vec2 feature extractor. Nothing touches disk.
Prosodic metrics
Computed with librosa in the same pass and returned with every prediction: speech rate, mean loudness, mean pitch, and energy variability.
Service
A FastAPI endpoint that takes the URL of an entry and returns the class logits plus the metrics as JSON, containerized with Docker so the Flutter client talks to a fixed contract and the model can be swapped underneath it.
Client
Flutter, handling recording, upload, and the history view.
