HomeRise Staff & Resident Portal

Google Developer Group · 2026 · team lead, 8 engineers

FlutterDartFirebase AuthFirestoreCloud StorageGitHub Actions

Overview

A production web application eight of us built for HomeRise, a nonprofit that runs residential care facilities. The portal handles staff authentication, daily roster scheduling, and resident record management across multiple facility sites.

The work that mattered here was rarely the hard part technically. Care staff rotate between sites, so who can see which resident's record changes by shift; the intake process had to stay usable by someone filling it in on a phone in a hallway; and the client's requirements kept moving as they discovered what they actually wanted, which is normal and which the architecture had to absorb rather than resist.

My part was the delivery side — sprint planning, client check-ins, splitting the work across eight engineers, and getting to an on-time handoff. The application is private-facing, so there is no public link.

How it works

Portal
The main staff portal is a Flutter application, structured around a modular page system with centralized routing. The team settled on that shape deliberately: with requirements still moving, we wanted adding a screen to stay a small change rather than a refactor.
Intake form
A separate form written in plain HTML and JavaScript, kept outside the Flutter app so it loads fast on a phone and can be handed to someone without an account.
Auth
Firebase Auth, with roles driving what each staff member can see — the scheduling views and the resident records are gated differently.
Data
Firestore holds staff and resident documents and streams them in real time, so a roster edit made at one site shows up without a refresh. Cloud Storage holds facility assets.
Automation
Daily wellness-check emails go out from a GitHub Actions workflow on a cron schedule. It is not the textbook place to put a recurring job, but it let us ship one without standing up and billing a server for a task that runs once a day — a reasonable trade for a nonprofit handover.
